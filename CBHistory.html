History of Cooper Black

A brief history of the Cooper Black typeface.

Designed by Oslwad Cooper in 1922, Cooper Black has been used for everything from store fronts to school posters. The typeface is round and soft, almost bouncy, and can be used to convey a sense of fun, jovality, or youth.

It's designer was a very popular hand lettering artist in the early 1900's. He lived in Chicago where he designed typefaces for commercial or ad use. During that time, he began workin on the larger 'Cooper' font family that Cooper Black would eventually be apart of. Cooper Black is the extra-bold twidth of the Cooper typeface, and when I say it's extra-bold, I really do mean extra-bold. It was really pushing limits in it's time.

Cooper Black blew up after it's conception, and while it's had eras of decline, it continually comes back into style. For example, after it's initial popularity in the 1920's, it had a slump during the Modernist movement, but after that came back into popularity during the Counterculture era of the 1960-70's. Since then, it's remained a staple to designers everywhere

Needless to say, Cooper Black has established itself as an absolute classic typeface. Once you know it's history it makes a lot more sense why this type is used for nearly everything under the sun.
